The script

Three sources are cut together: DECK slides exported from the pitch deck, SITE screen recordings of clash-fit.vercel.app, and LIVE footage of a real set on a real phone. The rule of the edit is that the live footage carries the proof and the other two carry the explanation — never the other way round.

Production notes

Framing

  • Shoot the phone side-on to the athlete, exactly as the app tells you to. If the video shows bad framing, the first thing a judge learns about the product is that it is fussy.
  • Whole body in frame, feet included. A cropped foot on camera undermines the ninety-degree field-of-view argument you make at 2:25.
  • Keep the phone still. Prop it; do not have someone hold it.

Light

  • Even, front-ish light. The pose model degrades in low light and you do not want to demonstrate that on camera.
  • Avoid a bright window behind you — it silhouettes the body and the tracking suffers.

Audio

  • Record the voiceover separately, indoors, close to the mic. Do not try to capture narration while training.
  • Leave the app's own audio in under the voiceover on the rep beats — the count and the haptic click sell it.

Edit

  • No speed ramps on the reps. If the footage is sped up, the movement stops looking like the thing being measured.
  • Hold the fatigue curve. It is the one image in the video that no other team has.
  • End on black with the wordmark and the URL. No music sting over the final line.
If you are forced under 90 seconds Keep beats 1, 2, 3, 4 and the sign-off. Cut the coach, privacy, multiplayer and beyond-the-game beats entirely rather than shortening all of them — four beats told properly beats nine beats rushed. The fatigue curve stays in every version.
