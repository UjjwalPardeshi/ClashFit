The loop

Reps in.
Damage out.

One still second calibrates you. The first rep sets your baseline, so everything after is scored against you.

  1. 01
    Prop the phoneSide-on, whole body in frame. A rep the camera could not see is never counted.
  2. 02
    Every rep lands a hitFour sub-scores grade every rep. The hit lands inside 100 ms, with a sound — so it works face-down.
  3. 03
    It reads the fadeYour output decays as you tire. The boss reads the decay and answers it.

The part nobody else has

It knows when you're gassed.

Not a difficulty slider. Not a self-reported effort rating. Three measured quantities, per rep, on the phone, against your own first three reps.

  • Velocity lossHow much slower the lifting half of the rep has become.
  • ROM collapseRange of motion shrinking rep over rep — depth going away before you notice.
  • Pause growthThe gap between reps stretching out. The most human tell there is.

And it never punishes you for it. The boss staggers as you fade, and the mercy rule guarantees you an ending.

Beyond the game

Not everything
here is a fight.

The camera that referees a boss fight is only a camera that can see a body. Four of the five below never involve a boss at all — and none of them ask you to wear anything.

  • 01

    The alarm you cannot dismiss from bed

    Set the alarm, then set its price: five push-ups, fifteen squats, a thirty-second plank. It keeps ringing until the camera has counted them, graded the way a boss fight is graded. A wiggle under the blanket counts as nothing. An emergency dismiss is always there, held five seconds and logged.

    Needs the native app
  • 02

    A daily budget for the app you keep opening

    Give one app a budget — twenty minutes of the feed you lose evenings to. Past it, it stays shut until you pay in reps. Same grading, so the price cannot be faked, and the budget is yours to set or delete on any day you want it gone.

    Needs the native app
  • 03

    Sixty seconds, every fifty minutes

    A desk timer for the eight hours nobody trains in. It asks for one minute: ten squats, or a stretch held long enough to count. It is the smallest honest unit of movement, and the camera is the only thing standing between you and skipping it.

    Camera only
  • 04

    What your neck did all day

    While you work, a single frame every few minutes, scored for neck flexion and shoulder height. One number at the end of the day and a curve across the week. The frames are read and discarded — nothing is stored, and nothing is a diagnosis.

    Camera only
  • 05

    Breathing you do not have to be trusted on

    After the set, six breaths a minute for two minutes. The phone watches your chest and shoulders rather than asking whether you kept to the pace, and tells you the rate you actually held. Recovery, measured the same way the training is.

    Camera only

Nothing here uploads. Nothing here is a diagnosis.

The device

Five pieces of the phone. No accessories.

No band, no strap, no chest belt, no gym machine reporting to an app. Everything ClashFit measures, it measures with hardware already in your hand — and the whole loop closes on that hardware.

  • Front camera The refereeThirty frames a second, thirty-three body landmarks. It is the only sensor the game needs — and a rep the camera could not see is never counted.
  • GPU Grading at camera rateThe pose landmarker runs on the GPU delegate, which is what leaves the CPU free to run the rep machine, the fatigue model and the fight at the same time.
  • Silicon A language model with no networkBetween sets, eleven numbers go to Gemma 3n running locally. It never runs while the camera does — the phone does one hard thing at a time, on purpose.
  • Haptics The rep you feelEvery counted rep is confirmed in the hand, so the screen is optional. It is what makes eyes-free sets possible at all.
  • Speaker Coaching out loudTempo cues and the coach's line are spoken by the phone. In Tempo Trial you never look at it — you are on the floor, face down.

None of this needs a flagship to run. But grading at camera rate and answering between sets without a network is where the 15's silicon stops being a spec sheet.
